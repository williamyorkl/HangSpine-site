What “grip age” means here

“Grip age” is a communication device, not a biological measurement. We compare your reading with average dynamometer values for your sex and age group, then show the age band whose average is closest. A 45-year-old with a 50 kg reading lands near the average 20–29 male; that does not mean their body is 25. It means their grip is strong relative to their age group.

If you enter a dead hang instead, the tool cannot produce a grip age from a dynamometer table — it shows an approximate hang percentile and level instead. Those are different tests; see dead hang benchmarks for the full explanation.

How the percentile is estimated

  • Grip strength: age/sex averages from published dynamometer norms (rounded, Bohannon-style reference data). We model a normal distribution with a standard deviation of about 20% of the mean and report an approximate percentile.
  • Dead hang: the same descriptive bands used in our hang calculator; the percentile is interpolated within the beginner → elite bands.
  • Not clinical: neither method is a diagnostic test. Grip strength is associated with health outcomes in cohort studies, but an individual reading needs context.

What to do with the number

Use it as a baseline, then train and re-test in 8–12 weeks. For grip strength, a dynamometer is the better tracker; for hanging performance, use a consistent hang test. If you want to improve grip, our grip standards guide and grip calculator go deeper. If the number is unexpectedly low or one side is much weaker, a clinician or physio is the right next step — not a web percentile.

FAQ

Is grip age real?

No. It is a population comparison, not a biological age. We use it because “you’re around the average 40s” is easier to understand than a z-score, but it is still an approximation.

Which hand should I test?

Test both, then use the average or your dominant hand consistently. A sudden large asymmetry deserves a professional assessment.

Can I use a dead hang instead of a dynamometer?

Yes, but it answers a different question: hang endurance, not peak grip force. The tool switches to a hang percentile and level.

Why does my percentile change between tests?

Grip is sensitive to sleep, fatigue, warm-up, hand position and effort. Test under the same conditions and look at the trend, not one reading.

Does a high percentile mean I’m healthy?

No. Grip is one marker among many. It does not diagnose or rule out any condition.
