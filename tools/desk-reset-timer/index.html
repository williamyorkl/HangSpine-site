Desk Reset Timer

A 60-second movement break for desk days. Hang if you have a bar; use desk mode if you don’t. Start, follow the prompts, stop at pain.

Why 60 seconds

Desk days compress the spine, shorten the chest and hip flexors, and keep the shoulders in the same position for hours. A 60-second break will not undo a day of sitting, but it is a reliable movement anchor: short enough to do between meetings, long enough to change position and breathe. The habit matters more than the perfect stretch.

How to use it

  • With a bar: 10 seconds setup (grip, feet off floor), 30 seconds relaxed hang, 20 seconds step down and one doorway chest stretch. Keep the hang easy; this is not a max test.
  • At the desk: 10 seconds sit tall, 15 seconds neck/shoulder range, 20 seconds chest/hip stretch, 15 seconds slow breathing. No bar required.
  • When: after a meeting, before lunch, or at the end of the workday. One fixed cue beats “when I remember”.

What it is not

It is not a cure for back pain, a substitute for strength training, or a reason to ignore symptoms. If you have spine, shoulder, or nerve symptoms, get assessed. The timer is a prompt to move, not a medical protocol.

FAQ

What if I don’t have a pull-up bar?

Use the “At the desk” mode. It gives you a 60-second neck, shoulder, chest and hip sequence without equipment.

Should I hang for the full 30 seconds?

Only if it feels easy. Use a chair so your feet share the load, or shorten the hang. The timer is not a max test.

Can I do this more than once a day?

Yes, if it feels good. Two or three 60-second breaks across a desk day is a reasonable habit; stop if you get sore.

Does it replace exercise?

No. It is a movement snack. Strength work, walking and sleep still do the heavy lifting.

What if I have shoulder pain?

Use the desk mode and avoid overhead positions until you have been assessed. Pain is a stop signal.
