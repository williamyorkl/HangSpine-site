How to run the plan

  • Three sessions a week, at least one rest day between them (e.g. Mon / Wed / Sat).
  • Session A — volume: four sets at rising percentages of this week's target. Rest 2 minutes between sets.
  • Session B — repeatable work: three comfortable sets at ~70% of target. These should feel easy; easy is the point.
  • Session C — test: one max attempt at the week's target, then two back-off sets at 60%.
  • Deload week (6–8 week plans): lighter sets, no max test. Boring on purpose — tendons adapt slower than muscles.
  • Rules that keep it working: full grip, no swinging; stop the set when form goes, not when grip screams; if you miss a week's target twice, repeat the week instead of forcing it.

Why ~8% a week?

Beginners often double their hang in the first two months, so 8%/week is deliberately on the conservative side — the plan's job is to keep you training next week, not to flatter you this week. Grip endurance responds quickly, but the forearm tendons that make hanging comfortable adapt on their own slower schedule; a modest weekly target plus one deload keeps both curves pointed the right way. If you blow past a target, celebrate, jump ahead a week, and let Session B stay easy.

Frequently asked questions

How fast can I improve my dead hang?

Most beginners double their max in 4–8 weeks of three easy sessions a week. The gains then slow — that's normal, and it's when plan and consistency matter more than effort.

How often should I train hangs?

Three sessions a week with rest days between. Daily max attempts are the classic way to stall and pick up elbow niggles.

What's a deload week?

A planned easier week — lighter sets, no test — letting grip and connective tissue catch up while you keep the habit alive. Plans of 6+ weeks include one around week 4.
