How to test your max hang properly

  1. Use a bar you can grip fully, at a height you can reach without jumping.
  2. Grab with an overhand (pronated) grip, roughly shoulder-width.
  3. Feet off the ground, arms straight, shoulders relaxed — no kipping, no swinging.
  4. Time from feet-up to let-go. One honest attempt after warm-up is enough; don't test to failure repeatedly.
Safety: stop if you feel sharp shoulder or elbow pain. If you have shoulder hypermobility or a diagnosed spine condition, ask a physical therapist first — and see our evidence review on dead hangs & scoliosis before adding hanging to a spine-care routine.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a beginner be able to hang?

Most healthy newcomers manage 10–30 seconds on their first tries. Around 20 s (men under 40) / 15 s (women under 40) is a normal starting point, and consistent practice usually doubles it within 4–8 weeks.

What is a good dead hang time?

45–90 seconds is a solid intermediate level for men under 40 (35–70 s for women); 90+ seconds is advanced. Remember these are fitness benchmarks, not medical standards.

Do dead hangs help scoliosis?

Hanging gives temporary decompression and stretch, and can be a useful, low-barrier habit — but current evidence does not show that hanging alone reduces a scoliosis curve. First-line conservative care is clinician-guided scoliosis-specific exercise (e.g. Schroth/PSSE). Read the full evidence review.

Are long dead hangs bad for shoulders?

For most healthy shoulders, no. With hypermobility or instability, keep light shoulder engagement (an “active” hang) and stop if it hurts. When in doubt, ask a PT.

Where this data comes from

Hang-time bands are descriptive fitness benchmarks synthesized from calisthenics coaching practice; grip values are rounded from published normative dynamometer data (Bohannon et al., reference values for grip strength). This tool is educational, not medical advice.
