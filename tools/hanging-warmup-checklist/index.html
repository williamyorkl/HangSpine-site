Hanging warm-up checklist

Ninety seconds between walking to the bar and loading your full bodyweight through cold grip tendons. Tick as you go, print it for the garage, and make the elbow niggles a story you tell, not one you live.

The 90-second routine — tick each box, then grab the bar.

Why ninety seconds is the right dose

Warm-up science keeps confirming an awkward truth: beyond getting tissue warm and joints cued, elaborate pre-workout rituals mostly burn time and attention. Hanging needs exactly three things before full load — the shoulder moving, the forearm flexors awake, and the nervous system reminded of the pattern. That's shoulder circles, fist work plus a light pinch, and two scapular pulls: about ninety honest seconds. The step people skip is the fifth, and it's the one that matters most — the first hang at half effort — because tendon complaints almost never come from the third hang onward. They come from hang number one, full effort, cold.

When to modify

  • Cranky shoulders: swap circles for wall slides; keep the scapular pulls — they're usually the medicine, not the problem.
  • Morning-only elbow pain: extend the towel work to 30s each side for a week; forearm tendons like longer, gentler visits.
  • Coming back after weeks off: do the list twice, then treat your first session as week one of the planner, not the week you left.
  • Over 50 or diagnosed bone/spine conditions: this list assumes a healthy baseline — see our over-50 guide and the condition-specific ones it links for the extra steps that apply to you.
Scope: general fitness education, not medical advice — persistent elbow or shoulder pain deserves a professional look, not a longer checklist.

Frequently asked questions

Do I really need to warm up for hanging?

Ninety seconds, yes: hanging is full-bodyweight through cold grip tendons — the classic elbow-niggle recipe. This list is the minimum effective dose, not ceremony.

What's the first-hang-at-half rule?

Session opener stays at ~50% of today's plan. Tendon complaints overwhelmingly come from hang #1 at full effort, cold.

Grip warm-up without equipment?

Fist open-close ×10 per hand, then a 15–20s towel wring or pinch. Blood flow, not fatigue.
