    What this score measures

    Two inputs, two jobs. Hang time is a proxy for grip endurance and shoulder tolerance; strict pull-ups are the strength expression. The score gives hang 50 points and pull-ups 50 points, because a first pull-up usually needs both a base and a pulling stimulus. It does not measure technique, body composition, pain, or how much you actually want it.

    Bodyweight and grip strength are optional context. Bodyweight helps choose assisted variations in the plan; grip is shown as a reference, not folded into the score. A hang is not a dynamometer test, and a pull-up is not a diagnosis.

    How the score is calculated

    • Hang (0–50): 30 seconds earns the full 50; less is prorated. Thirty seconds is a practical base for starting pull-up work, not a universal standard.
    • Pull-ups (0–50): 0 = 0, 1 = 15, 2 = 25, 3 = 35, 4 = 42, 5 = 46, 6+ = 50. The jump from 0 to 1 is the biggest single step.
    • Stage: under 30 Foundation; 30–54 Building; 55–79 Nearly there; 80+ Ready for reps. If you can already do one strict rep, the plan shifts to “next rep” work.
    • Weak link: if your hang is under 20 seconds, the plan starts with grip endurance. If the hang is fine but pull-ups are 0, it starts with scapular control, rows and negatives.

    How to test without fooling yourself

    1. Warm up for 90 seconds — arm circles, scapular pulls, one easy 10-second hang. Our warm-up checklist is free.
    2. Test the hang once: full grip, feet off the floor, relaxed shoulders, stop when the grip opens. One honest attempt is enough.
    3. Test pull-ups strict: dead hang start, chin over bar, no kipping; stop when form breaks. If you can’t do one, enter 0.
    4. Re-test every 4 weeks, not every session. Max tests are stressors; train in between.

    What to do with the plan

    Follow the plan for 4 weeks, then re-test. If the score moves but the weak link stays the same, keep the plan; if nothing moves, change one variable (volume, assistance, or recovery), not five.     FAQ

    Is this score accurate?

    It is a transparent planning heuristic, not a validated prediction. It is built to show the next useful step, not to tell you exactly when you will get a pull-up.

    What if I can already do pull-ups?

    Enter your strict rep count. The tool switches to “next rep” mode and gives volume/strength work instead of a first-rep progression.

    What if I can’t hang for 10 seconds?

    Use a chair so your feet share the load, build to 3 × 15 seconds, and re-test. A low hang score is a starting point, not a failure.

    Why is bodyweight optional?

    It doesn’t change the score; it helps choose assisted variations and load progression. If you don’t know it, skip it.

    Should I train to failure?

    No. Leave 1–2 reps in reserve on most sets; test only every 4 weeks. Failure is a tool, not a daily habit.
