Scoliosis appointment checklist

The appointment where you learn "you have scoliosis" lasts twenty minutes and determines months of decisions. Walk in with the right questions and it goes dramatically better. Pick your track, tick as you prepare, print, and bring it.

Why a checklist changes the appointment

Patient-education groups make the same observation: people who arrive with written questions leave with more answers, better recall, and fewer "I forgot to ask" regrets — and clinicians themselves recommend preparing a list before a scoliosis consultation [1] [2]. Mayo's diagnosis overview shows why preparation pays: the visit compresses history, exam, imaging, and a treatment conversation into very little time [3]. The questions below are organized the way the appointment actually unfolds.

Reading the answers

Three terms carry most of the meaning. The Cobb angle — how much curve, which direction, where (our primer explains it in one paragraph). Progression — whether and how fast it's expected to change, which drives the monitoring interval. And for adults, stenosis — whether the narrowing that causes leg symptoms is present (our degenerative scoliosis guide covers that pairing). If any answer feels rushed, the safest follow-up in medicine works here too: "Can you say that in plain words?"

Scope: this checklist is education, not medical advice, and it doesn't replace anything your clinician says — it just helps the conversation happen. New numbness, weakness, or balance changes deserve prompt attention regardless of any scheduled appointment.
