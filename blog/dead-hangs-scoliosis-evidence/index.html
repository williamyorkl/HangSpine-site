Dead hangs and scoliosis: what the evidence actually says

If you have a curved spine and you've seen videos promising that hanging from a bar will “fix” it — or the opposite claim that hanging is useless — this page is for you. We build a dead-hang app, and we're still going to tell you what hanging can't do.

By William, developer of HangSpine · Reviewed sources listed at the end · Nov 3, 2026
TL;DR

Dead hangs give your spine a temporary, gravity-driven stretch — real, pleasant, low-risk for most people. There is no good evidence that hanging alone reduces a scoliosis curve. One small 2024 study of hanging plus muscle-energy techniques reported short-term improvements in teens with idiopathic scoliosis — interesting, not conclusive. The evidence-based backbone of scoliosis care remains clinician-guided scoliosis-specific exercise (Schroth/PSSE), monitoring, and bracing when indicated. Hanging earns its place as an adjunct habit: decompression, shoulder health, grip strength, and something you'll actually do daily because it takes 60 seconds.

First, what scoliosis is (briefly)

Scoliosis is a sideways spinal curvature of 10° or more, measured on X-ray as the Cobb angle, usually with some vertebral rotation. About 80% of cases are adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS); in adults, degenerative “de novo” curves are remarkably common — one study found mild thoracic curves in roughly a quarter of people over 50 [1]. New to the vocabulary? Our two-minute primer covers types, angles and red flags.

What a dead hang actually does to your spine

Hanging puts your body weight through traction: it unloads compressed discs, stretches lats and the shoulder girdle, and lets the thoracic spine open under gravity. The decompression is real but temporary — the kind of relief you feel standing tall after a long hang, not a structural change. That's also true of traction tables in clinics: symptom relief and mobility, not curve correction [2].

Beyond the stretch, hanging builds grip strength — one of the better-studied general health markers — plus shoulder range of motion and scapular control. These are genuine benefits; they just aren't scoliosis treatment.

What the research says — including the study hanging fans cite

Searching PubMed for “hanging” and “scoliosis” returns very little, which is itself the finding. What exists:

  • Hanging + muscle energy techniques (2024). A Turkish research group tested adding hanging exercise to muscle-energy technique in adolescents with idiopathic scoliosis and reported short-term improvements in outcome measures [3]. Note what this is: a small, immediate-effects study of a combined protocol — a promising thread, not proof that hanging corrects curves.
  • Exercise therapy for scoliosis is legitimate — at the PSSE level. The strong results belong to scoliosis-specific programs. A 12-month study of a supervised Schroth program in braced adolescents significantly improved curve severity and quality of life [4], and recent meta-analytic work has found structured exercise therapy comparable to bracing at six months for moderate AIS on Cobb angle and quality-of-life outcomes [5].
  • Traction research overall is mixed — some symptom relief, no reliable structural change. Hanging is self-traction; expect the same pattern.
  • Cautious reviews agree. Independent calisthenics and physio summaries of the evidence conclude there isn't enough science to claim dead hangs change a scoliosis curve, while acknowledging them as a reasonable general practice [6]. We agree with that framing.

If you have scoliosis and want to hang: a sensible protocol

  1. Clear it. Mild stable curve and pain-free shoulders: proceed gently. Hypermobility, instability, pain, progression, or spinal hardware: ask your surgeon or PT first.
  2. Start short. 2–3 hangs of 10–20 seconds, full rest between, 1–2 minutes total per day.
  3. Choose your style. Passive hang (fully relaxed shoulders) for maximum stretch; active hang (shoulders lightly engaged) if your shoulders are loose or pinch.
  4. Stay symmetrical. A neutral, double-arm hang doesn't know your curve pattern — which also means it won't correct it. Curve-specific loading is Schroth territory.
  5. Log it and watch the trend. Consistency beats heroics: total weekly hang minutes matter more than one long max. (Shameless plug: HangSpine counts hangs automatically on Apple Watch — mount the bar and it starts, let go and it stops.)
  6. Stop signals: sharp shoulder/elbow pain, tingling in the arms, or next-day discomfort that doesn't fade. Any of these → back off and consult.

The backbone of evidence-based care hasn't changed

For mild curves: observation. For moderate curves in growth: scoliosis-specific exercise and, where indicated, bracing — the SOSORT conservative-care community publishes the guidance clinicians follow. For adults: symptom-driven care, exercise, and monitoring. Surgery remains rare and for specific indications. If someone tells you a single exercise replaces that ladder of care, they're selling something.

What this means in practice

If you have scoliosis and want to try hanging, treat it as a small, testable add-on rather than a treatment. Three practical rules:

  • Ask before you hang if your curve is progressing, painful, post-surgical, or you have shoulder instability. A physiotherapist or spine clinician can tell you whether traction is appropriate for your curve and hardware status.
  • Keep the dose boring. One to three easy hangs a day, 10–30 seconds each, is enough to test tolerance. Chase consistency, not a bigger stretch.
  • Measure the response, not the curve. Track pain, stiffness and function over two to three weeks. If symptoms worsen, stop and reassess; if nothing changes, you've lost nothing but a few minutes.

The honest summary: hanging may feel good and may help you move more, but it does not replace PSSE, bracing or medical monitoring. Use it as an adjunct, and keep the people who know your spine in the loop.

FAQ

Can dead hangs fix scoliosis?

No. No quality evidence shows hanging alone reduces a curve. It's a reasonable adjunct — temporary decompression, stretch, grip — alongside real scoliosis care.

Is hanging safe with scoliosis?

Usually, for mild stable curves with healthy shoulders. Caution with hypermobility/instability (keep shoulders lightly engaged), pain, progressing curves, or post-surgical hardware — ask your clinician.

What's the single best-evidenced exercise approach?

PSSE/Schroth from a certified therapist — individualized to your curve pattern. See our exercise library for context on general moves.

How long should I hang each day?

Start with 1–2 easy minutes total; build toward whatever your shoulders and grip tolerate comfortably. Benchmarks: dead hang calculator →
